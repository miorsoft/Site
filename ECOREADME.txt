This is a simulation of living beings.
It simulates a small, evolving ecosystem.
Brief explanation:
Each creature moves based on the inputs and outputs of its own neural network.
The neural network's inputs are derived from the creature's field of vision, supplemented by its current energy level, stomach fullness, and whether it is currently "under attack" or "pregnant."
The field of vision is divided into sectors, each indicating the amount of food (meat or plant matter) present, as well as the presence of other creatures (male or female).
The neural network's outputs determine the creature's speed and direction (steering).
Creatures are capable of reproduction.
There are males (yellow) and females (pink).
When a male and a female come into contact, the female may become pregnant (a state indicated by a highlight and a "swollen" appearance).
After a certain period, if a pregnant female has more than 2/3 of her maximum energy, she produces offspring; the offspring's genes are a combination of those from the mother and the father. If the female is pregnant but her energy drops below 1/3, the pregnancy is terminated.
The mother transfers a portion of her own energy (determined by a specific gene) to her offspring.
Minor mutations may occur in the genes of the offspring.
Creatures consume energy as they move, which they can replenish by feeding.
When a creature's energy runs out, it dies and turns into food (meat), which eventually decomposes into plant based food.
Plant based food grows autonomously.
If the total amount of plant matter falls below a certain threshold, new plants are added at random locations.
In addition to speed and direction, the neural network's outputs also determine a creature's aggression (indicated by red eyes) and its attack behavior.
If the genetic difference between two creatures (of the same sex) that come into contact exceeds a certain threshold (defined by a gene) and the neural network's "attack" output is active, the aggressor creature will inflict damage upon the other.

Tips:
- If you start with "primitive" creatures, keep in mind that it takes some time to see evolutionary results; be patient during the initial stages. (In the new version, a set of already-evolved creatures—randomly selected from the "Creatures" folder—is loaded upon startup.)
- To run the simulation at maximum speed, select "Max Speed" and minimize the window. Reopen it after a few minutes to check on progress.
- After a long session, save the "oldest" creatures (those that have gone through the most generations) or any others you prefer. (You can reuse them later, or in a different session, by using the "Add Creature" or "Repopulate" buttons, which will retrieve them from the "Creatures" folder.)
- You can load or save the entire current simulation pool at any time. (This uses subfolders within the "POOLS" directory.)
- Use the mouse wheel to zoom in or out.
- To move a creature, hold down the right mouse button and drag.

- Speeded up and refined the bushes drawing process.
- Brain Changes: Activation Function changed from TANH (Hyperbolic Tangent) to ELU (Exponential Linear Unit). This only applies to hidden neurons. TANH is retained for output neurons.
This sometimes causes poor backward compatibility with Creatures from previous versions, especially those with complex brains (with many hidden nodes). However, as far as non-complex brains (with few hidden nodes) are concerned, backward compatibility remains good. (Since most connections are input-to-output, and the activation function of the outputs, as mentioned, has remained unchanged (TANH).)
I initially considered creating two versions, one for Tanh and one for ELU, but after doing some testing, I noticed that even some Creatures with complex brains react well to the new Activation Function. Furthermore, having two versions would have complicated things too much. So, precisely, since many creatures, even if generated by training with TANH, respond quite well with ELU, I decided to maintain a single version of ECOSYSTEM anyway.
